The question this analyses answers

Maryland's 28 ENOUGH grantees serve 106 census tracts. The state has 92 Judy Center Early Learning Hubs. A grantee is a candidate for a Judy Center when its served tracts contain at least one elementary or combined school but no Judy Center is currently sited there. Per GOC guidance the operational filter is presence of an elementary in a JC-free tract, not a CPG threshold — CPG is shown alongside each candidate as information.

Three categories

Already covered —

Grantees whose served tracts already contain a Judy Center. No new application needed.

Catchment overlap —

No in-tract Judy Center, but a Judy Center's attendance zone overlaps a served tract by ≥ 5%. Coordination opportunity — may not require a new application.

Needs Judy Center —

No in-tract Judy Center and no overlapping catchment. Primary candidates for new Judy Center applications.

Named-overlap exemplars

Four grantees whose names already reference their host Judy Center — these validated the spatial-join correctness during development: City of Cumberland ↔ South Penn Elementary, One Annapolis ↔ Georgetown East Elementary, San Mar ↔ Bester Elementary, Frederick LMB ↔ Waverley / Hillcrest / Butterfly Ridge Elementary.

Methodology

The question

Which of Maryland's 28 ENOUGH grantees are good candidates to apply for a Judy Center Early Learning Hub? A grantee is a candidate when at least one elementary or combined school sits inside one of its served census tracts and no Judy Center is currently located there.

What this tool combines

  • The ENOUGH grantee roster. Supplied by the Governor's Office for Children: 28 grantees and the 106 census tracts they collectively serve.
  • The current Judy Center directory. Pulled from the Maryland Family Network's Judy Center Hubs Map, which Maryland Family Network maintains as the public-facing directory for MSDE. 92 Judy Centers as of April 2026.
  • Maryland's school points, school attendance zones, and census tract polygons. All from the Maryland Department of Planning's ENOUGH map service, the same layers that drive the public ENOUGH map at MDP.

How a grantee lands in each category

Each grantee is placed in exactly one of three buckets:

  • Already covered (15 grantees). At least one Judy Center sits inside a served census tract. No new application needed.
  • Catchment overlap (8 grantees). No Judy Center sits inside a served tract, but the school attendance zone of a Judy Center elsewhere overlaps a served tract by at least 5% of either the tract or the attendance zone. Indicates that an existing Judy Center is already serving students from inside the grantee's territory — a coordination opportunity rather than an application gap.
  • Needs Judy Center (5 grantees). No Judy Center inside a served tract and no overlapping attendance zone. These are the primary candidates for new Judy Center applications.

Reading the map

  • Tract polygons shade orange across all ENOUGH grantee tracts by default. Selecting a single grantee deepens the colour on that grantee's tracts and dims the rest for context.
  • Purple dots are every Judy Center in Maryland.
  • Blue stars appear when a selected grantee's tract contains a Judy Center.
  • Green stars appear when a selected grantee's tract is touched by a Judy Center's school attendance zone (the Judy Center itself is outside the grantee's polygons).
  • Red dots are the candidate elementary schools inside a selected grantee's tracts that are not yet Judy Centers. Dot size reflects each school's CPG (concentrated poverty grant) rate, but CPG is information, not a filter — every red dot is a viable candidate per GOC guidance.
  • Red dot with a dashed circle appears for grantees that have no traditional elementary or combined school inside their served tracts. The pipeline surfaces the closest non-JC elementary within 10 km of the tract edge as a fallback. Currently this applies only to Leaders Breeders Inc. (Sinclair Lane Elementary, ~0.5 km from their tract).

How Judy Centers were matched to map points

Each Judy Center on Maryland Family Network's directory was matched to MDP's school records using a tiered approach: exact name match within county, then a fuzzy name match, then an address match, then a fall-through using the directory's own coordinates. MDP's coordinates are preferred when available because some directory pins are off by enough to land in a different census tract. All 92 Judy Centers resolved cleanly.

Why CPG is shown but not used as a filter

Each school's CPG (concentrated poverty grant rate) is published by MDP as a three-year rolling average of students qualifying for free and reduced-price meals. Per GOC guidance for this analysis, the operational question is presence of an elementary school in a JC-free served tract, not whether that school clears a particular CPG threshold. CPG is displayed alongside each candidate to help reviewers prioritise visually, but every candidate elementary in a grantee's served tract is a viable candidate.

Caveats

  • A handful of Judy Centers serve attendance zones that span an entire small county (single-school counties on the Eastern Shore, for example). Those are flagged "county-wide" and excluded from catchment-overlap reporting because they don't represent meaningful proximity to any one grantee.
  • A school's CPG can be substantially higher than its census tract's child poverty rate. CPG measures the kids enrolled at the school; tract poverty measures the kids living in the tract. Open-boundary enrollment, magnet programs, and cross-tract attendance zones routinely create a gap. Belle Grove Elementary in Anne Arundel sits in a tract reporting 0% child poverty but reports 88% CPG, for instance.
  • Some pins on the Maryland Family Network directory are imprecise (Georgetown East Elementary's pin lands in the Chesapeake Bay, for example). This tool uses MDP's authoritative coordinates when available and falls back to the directory only as a last resort.
