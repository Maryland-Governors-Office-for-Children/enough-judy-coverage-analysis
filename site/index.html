What this analysis covers

Maryland's 28 ENOUGH grantees serve 106 census tracts. The state has 92 Judy Center Early Learning Hubs and 17 Patty Centers (Family Support Centers). This analysis breaks down which grantees have Judy Centers in their communities, which grantees have Judy Centers that serve their communities, which grantees are not served by Judy Centers, and which elementary schools are candidates to host new Judy Centers. It also maps every Patty Center and flags which ENOUGH grantees do not have one nearby.

Patty Centers

Patty Centers (formerly Family Support Centers) are community-based hubs for parents of children birth–3 — parents work toward their own goals (GED, job readiness, parenting skills) while their child receives early care on site. Unlike Judy Centers they are not housed in schools, so a grantee is counted as covered when a Patty Center sits inside a served tract and as "nearby" when one is within 5 km. Maryland Family Network runs 17 of them as MSDE's intermediary; the Blueprint funds 3 new sites per year through FY2030 at $330,000 each. Toggle them on the map with the Patty Centers control in the sidebar.

The three groups

Already covered —

A Judy Center is already operating inside one of the grantee's served census tracts. No new application needed.

Served by a nearby Judy Center —

No Judy Center sits inside the grantee's tracts, but one nearby draws students from inside them through its attendance zone. The grantee and the existing Judy Center are likely already reaching the same families.

Not served by a Judy Center —

No Judy Center inside the grantee's tracts, and no nearby Judy Center whose attendance zone reaches in. These grantees are the primary candidates for a new Judy Center.

Already-aligned grantees

Four grantees are named after their host Judy Center school or locality and operate in close partnership with it: City of Cumberland with South Penn Elementary, One Annapolis with Georgetown East Elementary, San Mar with Bester Elementary, and Frederick Local Management Board with Waverley, Hillcrest, and Butterfly Ridge Elementary.

Methodology

The question

Which of Maryland's 28 ENOUGH grantees are good candidates to apply for a Judy Center Early Learning Hub? A grantee is a candidate when at least one elementary or combined school sits inside one of its served census tracts and no Judy Center is currently located there.

What this tool combines

  • The ENOUGH grantee roster. Supplied by the Governor's Office for Children: 28 grantees and the 106 census tracts they collectively serve.
  • The current Judy Center directory. Pulled from the Maryland Family Network's Judy Center Hubs Map, which Maryland Family Network maintains as the public-facing directory for MSDE. 92 Judy Centers as of April 2026.
  • Maryland's school points, school attendance zones, and census tract polygons. All from the Maryland Department of Planning's ENOUGH map service, the same layers that drive the public ENOUGH map at MDP.

How grantees are sorted into the three groups

  • Already covered (15 grantees): a Judy Center sits inside one of the grantee's served census tracts.
  • Served by a nearby Judy Center (8 grantees): no Judy Center is inside a served tract, but the attendance zone of one elsewhere covers part of a served tract. Most often this means a Judy Center and an ENOUGH grantee are already reaching the same families even though the school sits across a tract boundary.
  • Not served by a Judy Center (5 grantees): no Judy Center inside a served tract and no nearby Judy Center whose attendance zone reaches in. These are the grantees most worth a conversation about a new Judy Center.

Reading the map

  • Orange tract polygons mark every ENOUGH grantee's served census tracts. Selecting a grantee deepens the orange on that grantee's tracts and dims the others for context.
  • Purple dots show every Judy Center in Maryland.
  • Blue stars appear when the selected grantee already has a Judy Center inside one of their served tracts.
  • Green stars appear when a Judy Center sits outside the selected grantee's tracts but its attendance zone reaches in.
  • Red dots are the elementary schools inside the selected grantee's tracts that aren't currently Judy Centers — the candidates for new Judy Center placement. Dot size reflects each school's CPG (concentrated-poverty) rate as a quick visual cue; all of them are viable candidates.
  • Red dot with a dashed circle appears when a grantee's served tracts contain no traditional elementary at all, in which case the closest elementary outside the tract is shown as a fallback. Currently this applies only to Leaders Breeders Inc., whose tract sits about half a kilometre from Sinclair Lane Elementary.
  • Teal squares are Patty Centers (Family Support Centers). They show statewide by default and can be toggled off in the map sidebar. When a grantee is selected, a Patty Center inside one of its tracts is drawn solid and one within 5 km gets a dashed outline.

Patty Centers

Patty Centers (formerly Family Support Centers) are community-based hubs for parents of children birth–3, run by Maryland Family Network as MSDE's intermediary at community sites — not inside schools. Because they have no school attendance zone, grantee proximity is measured as straight-line distance from the grantee's served-tract union to the Patty Center: a center inside a served tract counts as covered, one within 5 km counts as nearby, and otherwise the grantee has no nearby Patty Center. The Blueprint for Maryland's Future funds 3 new Patty Centers per year through FY2030 at $330,000 each, so the grantees with no nearby center are natural candidates for future expansion. Locations come from the same Maryland Family Network directory as the Judy Centers (the "(PC)"-tagged entries).

Matching Judy Centers to schools

Each Judy Center from Maryland Family Network's directory was paired with the corresponding school in MDP's records, working through name, address, and finally directory coordinates. MDP's coordinates are preferred when available because a few directory pins are off by enough to land in the wrong census tract. All 92 sites paired cleanly.

About CPG (concentrated poverty grant rate)

CPG is a three-year rolling average of the share of students at a school who qualify for free and reduced-price meals. The map shows it next to each candidate elementary as a visual prioritisation cue. It is not used as a filter for this analysis — what matters here is whether a grantee's tracts contain an elementary that isn't already a Judy Center.

A few things to keep in mind

  • A handful of Judy Centers serve attendance zones that cover most of a small county. Those are excluded from "served by a nearby Judy Center" reporting because covering an entire county doesn't really tell you anything about proximity to a particular grantee.
  • A school's CPG can be much higher than the child-poverty rate of the tract it sits in. CPG counts the students enrolled at the school; tract poverty counts the children living in the tract. Open-boundary enrollment and magnet programs routinely produce this gap. Belle Grove Elementary in Anne Arundel, for instance, reports 88% CPG in a tract whose published child-poverty rate is 0%.
  • A few pins on the Maryland Family Network directory aren't precise — Georgetown East Elementary's pin, for example, sits in the Chesapeake Bay. This tool uses MDP's authoritative coordinates when available so those pins don't throw off the analysis.
